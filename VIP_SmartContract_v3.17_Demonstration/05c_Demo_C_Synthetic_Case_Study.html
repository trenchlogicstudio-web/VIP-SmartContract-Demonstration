Assumptions · sources · where this can break
  • Concentration context: industry analyst estimates put roughly 30% of GGR with the top 1% of players — why one protected whale is a P&L line, not a CSR line.
  • Empirical anchor: peer-reviewed responsible-gambling research finds voluntary limit-setters at 2.92× the odds of remaining active a year later. That is what makes the modeled quarter believable.
  • The 84% vs 61% quarter retention pair is MODELED: it compares the incentive layer against a hard-stop-only baseline that loses 39% of stopped players in-quarter. It is a design comparison, not a field result, and every frame says so.
  • €5,100 preserved per limited VIP per quarter is the slice of the pre-committed budget still unspent when the policy fires — never the guest’s whole theoretical. The slider runs €2,000–€12,000 so a buyer can set it to their own VIP tier.
  • 70% non-gaming capture assumes integrated resort amenities; drag it to 40% of the preserved budget for a gaming-only property and watch the redirect shrink honestly.
  • Regulatory direction of travel — UK high-value customer rules, US affordability proposals — is context for why this ships now; it is not a model input. Above 90% of the limited-VIP cohort retained, the model prints its own warning beside the verdict, and the slider stops at 92%.
