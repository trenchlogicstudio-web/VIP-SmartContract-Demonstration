Model verdict

Assumptions · sources · where this can break
  • Concentration context: industry analyst estimates put roughly 30% of GGR with the top 1% of players — why one protected whale is a P&L line, not a CSR line.
  • The cited study: voluntary limit-setters had 2.92× the odds of remaining active a year later than players who set no limit. Both variants on this page already have a limit, so the study does not measure what the incentive adds.
  • The 84% vs 61% quarter retention pair is MODELED: it compares the incentive layer against a hard-stop-only baseline that loses 39% of stopped players in-quarter. It is a design comparison, not a field result, and stays an assumption until a pilot measures it. The slider runs from 40% so the model can show the incentive layer doing worse than the stop alone.
  • €5,100 preserved per limited VIP per quarter is the slice of the pre-committed budget still unspent when the policy fires — never the guest’s whole theoretical. The slider runs €2,000–€12,000 so an operator can set it to their own VIP tier.
  • 70% of the preserved budget issued as resort credit assumes integrated resort amenities; drag it to 40% for a gaming-only property and watch the credit shrink honestly. 80% is the ceiling: with the rollover at 20% of the preserved budget the engine accepts no more, and the remainder returns to the guest.
  • Issued is not redeemed. Resort credit becomes spend only when a guest uses it, and the rollover is credit toward future play, owed until the guest comes back. Until then both are an obligation of the resort. The redeemed share is an assumption: no redemption has been measured.
  • Regulatory direction of travel — UK high-value customer rules, US affordability proposals — is context for why this ships now; it is not a model input. Above 90% of the limited-VIP cohort retained, the model prints its own warning beside the verdict, and the slider stops at 92%.

Auer, Hopfgartner and Griffiths report the 2.92 odds ratio for voluntary limit-setting in Table 5. The quarter assumptions on this page are illustrative. Read the study.
