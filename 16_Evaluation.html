Replay your own past sessions

Before anything runs live, an operator can see what the policy would have cost on its own history. Export past sessions as a CSV with three columns: an anonymised guest key, the date written YYYY-MM-DD and the session’s net loss_eur, negative for a session the guest won. Optional columns: peak_loss_eur, the deepest the guest went during the session; budget_eur, a limit the guest actually set; and eligibility, the operator’s determination. A column that looks like personal data, such as a name or an e-mail address, is refused, because none is needed. The replay runs on the operator’s own computer and sends nothing anywhere.

npm run replay -- sessions.csv --budget typical:2 --json report.json --stops stops.csv

It applies the engine’s own stop line and settlement, under the same settings as the service, including the benefit ceiling and the future-play switch, and reports the stops, the gaming win forgone, the resort credit by venue, the future-play credit and the guests’ own balances, in total and by month. Past sessions have no pre-committed budget, so the budget is an assumption stated in every report: typical:2 gives each guest twice the median of the guest’s own losing sessions, so the line falls at the guest’s typical loss; fixed:20000 gives every guest the same budget. The result is the cost of the policy under that assumption, at face value. It is not a measurement of how guests behave once they have pre-committed; that is what the pilot below measures. Try it on the synthetic file that ships with the package:

npm run replay -- 13_VIP_MVP/samples/sessions-synthetic.csv

Measure a pilot

Primary question. Among consenting guests who meet the same operator eligibility criteria and have the same stop protection, does offering resort benefits change the probability of a later non-gaming visit within 90 days?

Use the first eligible stop per guest as day zero. Where the operator permits a comparison, assign guests once to the same Hard-Stop with or without the benefit offer. Keep limits, access to support, follow-up and observation windows the same. Eligibility withdrawals always take effect. Set sample size, review dates and stopping criteria before enrolment. With an observational comparison, report the association and explain differences in eligibility and prior visits.

Return to the resort
Count distinct assigned guests with at least one recorded non-gaming visit on days 1–90, divided by all assigned guests in that arm with a complete observation window. Use equivalent visit tracking in both groups, including visits without a credit redemption. Show both counts, the absolute difference in percentage points and its uncertainty. List incomplete follow-up separately.
Return to gaming
Report separately the share with a later gaming session over the same window. A restaurant or spa visit alone counts only toward the non-gaming outcome.
Resort economics
Reconcile credit issued, redeemed and outstanding. Compare actual cash receipts net of refunds, fulfillment costs and programme costs per assigned guest. Report credits used separately. Track liabilities for future-play credit and the amount due to guests. An issued credit is a liability and does not establish incremental revenue.
Guest protection
Report checked stops that overshot their line, attempted commitments still under the hold, withdrawn eligibility and any benefits issued after withdrawal. Review an overshoot or post-withdrawal issuance before continuing new offers. The operator retains its existing guest-support process in both groups.

The 84% and 61% in the synthetic quarter are model inputs. Demo C includes a downside scenario at 52% against 61%. No operator observations are included in this release.

How the interval is calculated

The absolute difference is the visit rate with the offer minus the visit rate with the stop alone. The 95% interval uses Newcombe’s independent-group method with Wilson limits. It describes sampling uncertainty. Selection differences, incomplete follow-up, repeat participation and property-level clustering require separate analysis. SAS FREQ, Newcombe confidence limits.
